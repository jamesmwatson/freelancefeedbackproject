# Methodology

## 1. Establish what can be recovered

Inventory the mailbox before interpreting it. Count messages and attachment types, identify repeated attachment content, record parsing failures and retain unresolved items. The original work used stable source relationships and resumable processing to make long runs inspectable.

The preserved inventory script streams through an MBOX, prefers one readable body representation and counts attachment payload hashes. Its reply-header grouping is an approximation of conversations. Its timing and token figures are planning assumptions, not measured model benchmarks.

## 2. Discover candidates

The project combined lexical rules, direction and sender filters, attachment structure, local model classification and source-level consolidation. Candidate discovery deliberately began broadly. Repeated delivery QA material and other low-value sources were then excluded from particular experiments.

A candidate means “worth examining”. It does not mean “verified feedback”. Experimental funnel counts described different populations and processing stages; they could not be used as final quality or recall measurements.

## 3. Diagnose extraction errors

Later extraction experiments separated stages to limit contextual leakage and checked whether proposed evidence could be located in the source. Quote diagnostics distinguished exact matches from whitespace, typography and punctuation differences, and from text that could not be located.

A located quote can still be irrelevant, quoted from someone else, or insufficient to support the proposed claim. These diagnostics check textual grounding; they do not replace contextual reading. The included matching excerpt retains this limited role.

## 4. Make human review authoritative

The project changed method when uncertainty about ground truth became more important than another extractor revision. Attachment verification and review of eligible incoming conversations established the authoritative decisions.

Attachment context helped prioritise conversation review, while body feedback was judged separately. Surrounding messages and quoted text were retained where needed to understand a conversation. Bulk controls made obvious negatives manageable. Uncertainty remained an explicit outcome.

Keep these dimensions separate:

| Dimension | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Body feedback | Does the correspondence itself contain useful feedback? |
| Attachment feedback | Does the attachment contain substantive feedback? |
| Template | Is the document a recurring form? |
| Showcase | Is the material useful for illustrating professional work? |
| Evidence pointer | Where is the specific supporting message or passage? |

A template can contain substantive feedback. A useful showcase example need not contain praise. An email delivering a feedback attachment need not itself contain feedback.

## 5. Freeze the decisions and their context

The completed project preserved source identities, threading, attachment relationships, review text, decisions, excerpts, notes, showcase metadata, automatic outputs and validation information in a full-fidelity snapshot. Portable tables complemented the databases. A later evidence-pointer amendment retained provenance rather than silently rewriting the frozen state.

The important property was reconstructability: a list of final labels alone could not explain what had been reviewed or how a conclusion was reached.

## 6. Synthesise for two purposes

The evidence register grouped material into defensible claims and stories for applications and interviews. The retrospective examined broader patterns and development over time. Both were derived from the completed reviewed evidence base; neither required another discovery pass.

The authority order was: original source; frozen human decisions and documented amendments; verified pointers and crosswalks; synthesis; automatic extraction output. A discrepancy should prompt verification at the appropriate layer.

## Limits

The correspondence archive reflects what was preserved and communicated, not every professional activity. Praise, criticism and silence are not sampled evenly. Repeated correspondence is not independent evidence, and scores from different evaluation schemes should not be pooled without justification. Completed review of an eligible population does not prove perfect extraction or eliminate human judgement errors.

This public edition demonstrates mechanics with invented records. It cannot independently validate the private career conclusions.
