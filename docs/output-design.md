# Output design and fictional worked example

All source text, people, dates, identifiers and decisions in this example are invented. They do not paraphrase an actual project or client exchange.

## Two outputs

| Output | Unit | Main use | Necessary constraint |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Application-facing register | A professional claim or story supported by one or more sources | CV tailoring, applications, interviews and STAR examples | State what the evidence supports and where it stops |
| Career retrospective | A pattern, period or development supported by multiple observations | Personal understanding and a coherent account of a career | Separate direct evidence from interpretation and acknowledge gaps |

## Follow one source through the workflow

The demo's message `demo-message-1` delivers `review-notes.txt`:

> Please see the attached review notes.

Its attachment contains:

> The revised shutdown step is clear. You identified that the source gave two different waiting times and asked for clarification before delivery. For future revisions, use the approved term consistently in the headings.

These are two separate review decisions. The email body is an administrative delivery note with no substantive feedback. The attachment contains both positive and developmental feedback. In the early viewer it can be marked `possible_feedback`; the invented verification below is a separate later-stage decision.

| Layer | Fictional record |
| --- | --- |
| Source message | `demo-message-1`, first occurrence of attachment `demo-attachment-1` |
| Attachment locator | `review-notes.txt`, paragraph 1 |
| Body decision | No useful body feedback |
| Attachment verification | Contains substantive feedback |
| Template flag | False |
| Showcase flag | True, independently of the feedback decision |
| Quote check | Exact location of “You identified that the source gave two different waiting times and asked for clarification before delivery.” |

Message `demo-message-2` repeats the same attachment payload. It supplies another occurrence, not a second independent reviewer endorsement.

## Example evidence card

**Title:** Clarifying an inconsistent instruction before delivery

**Defensible claim:** In this example, the practitioner identified conflicting source instructions and sought clarification before delivery.

**Direct evidence:** The attachment explicitly credits identification of two different waiting times and a clarification request. It also identifies a terminology consistency issue for future revisions.

**Evidence type:** Direct reviewer feedback, combining positive and developmental observations.

**Provenance:** `demo-message-1` → `demo-attachment-1` → `review-notes.txt`, paragraph 1. These are fictional identifiers local to the example. No real source hashes or message IDs are included.

**Limits:** The source does not establish the correct waiting time, the technical severity of the inconsistency, quantified impact, or a repeated pattern across projects. The reviewer reports the clarification request; the request itself is not included as a separate work product.

**Skills illustrated:** Source ambiguity detection; appropriate escalation; technical communication; learning from review.

**Possible application use:** An interview example about identifying an unclear requirement and checking it before completion.

**STAR skeleton:** Situation — a source instruction contained conflicting waiting times. Task — produce a clear version. Action — identified the conflict and requested clarification. Result — the reviewer described the revised step as clear; no further impact claim is established.

This card is a hand-authored illustration. The viewer does not create cards or make these judgements automatically.

## Retrospective structure

A private retrospective can organise verified evidence around:

1. The nature of the work and the coverage of the surviving evidence.
2. Phases supported by actual changes in responsibilities or evidence.
3. Recurring capabilities, with direct support and counterexamples.
4. Developmental feedback and observable responses to it.
5. Operational trust and repeated responsibilities.
6. What is directly established, what is inferred and what remains unknown.

A single card like the example above cannot establish a career trajectory. Several independent, dated observations would be needed before making a claim about development over time.
